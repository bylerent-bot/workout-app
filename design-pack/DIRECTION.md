# IRON CIRCUIT visual direction

Status: D1 leaning, 2026-08-03, Codex proposal for Patrick. The owner still selects the name and may redirect the identity.

## Ship this: Arcade Broadcast

This should feel like a black-market strength tournament televised from the back room of an arcade. The interface combines 16-bit cabinet graphics, sports broadcast hierarchy, and modern mobile ergonomics. Pixel art gives it identity. Crisp data typography gives it speed. Electric lime, hot red, and ice blue behave like cabinet lamps against a near-black steel shell.

Pure 8-bit everywhere would become visual static during a hard set. Arcade Broadcast keeps the soul and loses the friction. Display moments are pixelated and loud. Logging controls are blunt, current, and extremely legible. The result feels like a game without making the player fight a game skin.

The cabinet metaphor runs through the entire app:

- Top status rail = marquee and player HUD.
- Primary cards = inset cabinet screens with hard clipped corners.
- Progress bars = segmented power meters.
- Bottom nav = four cabinet controls: TRAIN, FUEL, RIVALRY, VAULT.
- Confirmations = score popups and combo language.
- Rest timer = full-width danger state, built to read from several feet away.
- DONE = victory screen, not a receipt.
- The object comparison = a share card called LOADOUT RECEIPT.

It should never cosplay as a generic neon cyberpunk product. No purple gradients, glass cards, thin wireframe icons, soft pill buttons, or decorative scanlines over body text. The cabinet is physical, dirty, and competitive.

## Alternate: Pure 8-bit Fight Card

The alternate commits to a strict 8-pixel grid, bitmap type at every scale, two-tone sprites, and bracket-like fight cards. It is instantly charming and cheaper to art-direct, but it loses legibility in dense set rows and risks feeling like a novelty after week three. Use this direction for share cards, badges, and seasonal event posters, not the full operating interface.

## Recommendation

Ship Arcade Broadcast. It carries the requested retro cabinet energy across every screen while preserving the ruthless speed of the existing workout flow. Use the pure 8-bit alternate as a limited-event skin for boss battles and monthly finales.

## Naming candidates

1. **IRON CIRCUIT** - Favorite. It connects lifting, competition, a closed group, and arcade electronics without sounding cute.
2. **REP RIVAL** - Says exactly what the product does and reads cleanly under an app icon.
3. **HEAVY HITTERS** - Competitive, loud, and naturally plural as the roster grows.
4. **LOADOUT** - A gaming term that also describes what is on the bar and what the player brought today.
5. **LAST REP** - Built-in tension and a natural phrase for challenges and notifications.
6. **IRON BOARD** - A high-score board for lifting, with a blunt old-gym sound.
7. **BEEF MODE** - Funny, profane-adjacent, memorable, and deliberately unserious.
8. **RACK ATTACK** - High arcade energy and instantly gym-specific.
9. **REP WAR** - Very direct rivalry language with room for seasons and campaigns.
10. **HEAVY SCORE** - Makes the scoring layer the product and works well as a verbal command.
11. **PRESS START** - The cleanest arcade phrase, with a lifting double meaning.
12. **BAR FIGHT** - Strongest attitude and best merch line, but more collision and moderation risk than IRON CIRCUIT.

## Identity lockup

Working lockup: `IRON / CIRCUIT` stacked in all caps, left aligned, with the I in CIRCUIT replaced by a vertical loaded bar. A small `P1` lamp sits at the top right. App-icon reduction uses `IC` over a four-segment lime power meter.

Voice examples:

- Session ready: `P1, GET YOUR ASS ON THE FLOOR.`
- Set logged: `+420 XP. CLEAN SHIT.`
- Missed target: `YOU LEFT 18 POINTS ON THE TABLE.`
- Rival lead change: `MIKE STOLE THE LEAD. FIX THAT.`
- Rest complete: `TIME. MOVE THE DAMN BAR.`
- Food target hit: `FUEL LOCKED. STOP EATING LIKE AN INTERN.`
- Safety or form review: `STOP. THE REP DOES NOT COUNT. FIX YOUR SHOULDER PATH.`

Profanity is seasoning, not every sentence. The app should sound like one funny, observant training partner, not a teenager mashing a swear button.
